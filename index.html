Academia And My Love-Hate story with it
"Metric Spaces"

This is my best and happyiest experience doing academic research and I'm still on it at this moment. There are tons of things that didn't worked like my idea of using hash functions to make a "locality signature" in order to speed up indexing/search time on large datasets, but at the same time I discovered some interesting things (to me at least).

One of those is an improvement for IQS (an algorithm to solve the Incremental Sorting problem) based on aproximate median selection methods and introspectie behaviour to flatten IQS's worst case running time published in 2015 and presented in a local conference. There are ton of work ahead, specially with the deterministic analysis of the algorithm that I've developed but dealing with a mixed deterministic/non-deterministic algorithm complexity is hard for me in this moment while struggling with my full-time studies and my work.

"Semantic Web"

I'm trying to implement some kind of three-valued logic into SPARQL 1.1 algebra evaluations in order to answer queries with a "I don't know' answer. It started as a course project but in the end I got hooked with it.

"My hate story"

There are cool teachers and lame teachers. The cool teachers encourage students, show them that there are no limits. Lame teachers try to use students as cheap research workpower and go the classroom to teach topics that they define themselves as experts knowing nothing more than pure theory. I know that theory is important but trying to solve problems with pure theory is like trying to satisfy your stomach just by reading the menu.

For things like that I've came to think of academa like some sort of mafia. Not all academics here do that though, there are some cool ones which I really love to work with them side-by-side.
