Mutable circuitry, the most weirdest idea that I ever had.
The past years I have been learning about artificial neural networks and I found neurogenesis as an interesting concept for me. You know, our brain stores information and learn by mutating the connections between their neurons, and sometimes a new neuron is born between them or sometimes is killed by one reason or another, who knows. But this is a problem when simulating that behiavour on a computer, we do not have infinite precision nor almost instant propagation time. Computers can be shit sometimes.

The problem with artificial neural networks it's their processing time, depending on how you organise the grid, to perform learning can be a very intensive operation, even with GPUs which can reduce the constant factor of their time complexity but sadly, not it's order. More over, ANNs doesn't have infinite precision as animal neurones have with their electric signals. Ok, maybe it's an analog problem and we are cursed because we want to synthetise their behiavour in a digital way... we humans are crazy sometimes but for now, that is the best that we can do.

Then, I realised that if the computing model changes for ANNs then there is a possibility to accelerate the computation time or at least to improve their precision by making a physic electronic replica of the ANN (ignoring background noise and such for now), but those cannot be implemented with a simple FPGA due to their digital nature beacuse of processing density.

There are FPAAs which implement analog blocks which someone can use to simulate them by changing their configuration of passive components but without a mixed mode it cannot implement the algorithms needed to perform learning operations. As far as I know, GPUs are used for the learning phase and FPGAs used for recognition and it works pretty well, makes sense right?.

Learning process in ANNs is not trivial as one can think, even simple ANNs like used in Self Organizing Maps are a complex thing to imagine in a hardware design, from the point of view of a programmer. We can replace a neuron with a set of operational amplifiers, resistors, capacitors, transistors and other things, but that is not usable by us, developers... well, we can but after an intense suffering. Specially when implementing the learning phase of a SOM, searching for the best matching unit , traveling all the circuitry and make all neurons aware of the results of their pairs and then finally taking a decision. It is not that hard though, since you can always propagate the result of each neuron... but wait, you need to persist the signal to learn. Now each neuron has a live signal expresing a value to be compared in order to find our best matching unit. And then you need to perform a radial search across the grid in order to perform prototype migrations... Fuck it.

Then the problem of making such hardware is the usability for final users. Imagining that such technology is a reality, then it is useless without a proper toolchain that programmers can use abstracting from low level details. Take Google's Tensor Flow as example, it abstracted many things making AI more friendly to non specialist users and it is cool and easy to use. Code should be meant for humans, not for machines.

There are ton of work about using FPGAs to implement neural network ( you can search in google about "FPGA neural networks" ), so using FPAAs in mixed mode to accomplish this is not a great asset, as they cannot contribute much for the meantime, and there is even work related to neurosynaptic chips (SyNAPSE chips) by IBM but for me, a mere student with no money and delayed two years such thing is not avaliable. I don't even have anybody to ask about it here. Yeah right, get involved but I do not have the time nor the resources to do so. I am just a hardware enthusiast, I have not taken any EDA course, nor a decent electronics one, all that I know is self taught by reading books and browing the internet (yeah, you can use it for more than just checking Facebook). I am even saving money to buy an FPGA to stop using simulators and then playing with an FPAA if I get enough level to fight against one.

I know that this makes no sense at all trying to implement both learning and recognition in the same hardware as well as the toolchain in a mixed mode FPGA if such technology to replicate human brain behiavour already exists. But I just want to know if there is a way to do it and if it is possible to implement it.

Would not you do the same thing if you are curious?

TL;DR: I want to know if there is a way to make calculations with way more precision than 128bits and to know if there is a way to create a circuit that can mutate by itself.
