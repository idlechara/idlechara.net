Academia And My Love-Hate story with it
"Metric Spaces (2015)"

Best and happyiest experience doing research and I'm still researching on that topic to the date . There are tons of things that didn't worked like my idea of using hash functions to make a "locality signature" in order to speed up indexing/search time on large datasets, but at the same time I discovered some interesting things (to me at least). It wasn't a failure for me, in fact, I've learned what things doesn't actually work on metric spaces indexing and algorithm development.

One of the byproducts is an improvement for IQS (an algorithm to solve the Incremental Sorting problem) based on aproximate median selection methods and introspective behaviour to flatten IQS's worst case running time published in 2015 and presented in a local conference. There is ton of work ahead, specially with the deterministic analysis of the algorithm but dealing with a mixed deterministic/non-deterministic algorithm complexity and struggling with my full-time studies and my work at the same time is hard at this moment.

"Semantic Web (2016)"

I'm also trying to implement some kind of three-valued logic into SPARQL 1.1 algebra evaluations in order to answer queries with a "I don't know' answer. It started as a course project but in the end I got hooked with it.

UPDATE! (2017): I just quit the research. One of the main difficulties lies on ARQ, the SPARQL query processor. And for the research to succeed I needed to modify it (more precisely, to make my own three-valued logic implementation) and the "guide professor" just wanted to "gather all the findings to document them". I'm out.
"My love-hate story (2016)"

There are cool teachers and lame teachers. The cool teachers encourage students, show them that there are no limits. Lame teachers try to use students as cheap research workpower and go the classroom to teach topics that they define themselves as experts knowing nothing more than pure theory. I know that theory is important but trying to solve problems with pure theory is like trying to satisfy your stomach just by reading the menu.

I usually get pissed when teachers treat students like inferior beings. I know may good and promising students that because of that posture they get discouraged to pursue an academical carreer, I'm one of those. I'm also get offended easily when someone tells me "Oh, and you how many courses have done? How many left you have till your memoria?". It feels like they think of us as free workers for you, not students not even persons. I want support too, I want to do things and I know that I can do it but with things as they are now, they have my hands tied to a wall.

If you ask me what is my posture regarding research is that research should be done with propuse, no just by mere "sense of discovery" because science is dead now thanks to the publishing fever and ISI/SCOPUS indexes. Because of that pressue to publish now academics/researchers doesn't care about the content anymore, now it's about who publishes more and faster.

This situation has killed science, not even being a philosophy anymore. Now there is no such thing as "pure love" involved while "making science".

But then if there is no love, at least there should be a propuse behind that has more sense than mere status, ranking or money... the desire to contribute to the world, to push their knowledge barriers further and to break it if it's possible. Because of that I'm not fond of research world as it is now, not at least in academia.

If you're reading this, please remember to make your research for fun or love. This is what we really need to change the world.

For things like that I've came to think of academa like some sort of mafia sometimes. Not all academics here do that though, there are some cool ones which I really love to work with them side-by-side and many others that I've met that I would love to work with them.

Competitive Programming:
Being the ACM-ICPC coach while you're still a student
I've been the coach for the teams that go to the 'ACM Inter Collegiate Programming Contest' since 2015, training two teams and two generations so far. Except for a few bumps with some of my school and university personnel (teachers) which give none and even negative support to this activity the road has been quite "peaceful".They want to send people to the competition but they do not care about training them nor giving them support, so I started to train contestants myself in order to change that panorama with little (?) results.

Despite all of that shit, I'm devoted to my students and more than winning the competition I want is to see them challenging themselves enjoying the experience, smiling while solving problems. I like my students, they are really a bunch of cool guys with tons of potential and I believe that education can make the world a better place for them.
A short letter to my students (ACM-ICPC - 2016)

Context - Open letter from the "ACM-ICPC 2016 - South America / South" team "Hot Singles In Your Area"

Due to strange circumstances one of the two teams that I was coaching ended in first place in "ACM-ICPC - South America South regionals" - to be more precise the name of the team was "Hot Singles In Your Area" -.

Making a short and entangled story short and easy, the team that was supposed to be the best got disqualified because unjustified reasons 15 minutes before starting the competition. Thanks to the staff they could still compete against other local teams but without score. Because of that our team obtained first place (otherwise we should be ended with 2nd place).

Our team feels that advancing to the first position due to that circumnstances is not fair so I as their coach somehow managed to steal the mic to give them in order to convey their feelings to the rest of the contestants.

I'm very proud of them, winning is not the most important thing in life and they showed to me a pleasant gesture of true honesty at the award ceremony, thanks to that I feel now that my duties as coach are complete to them, making me feel like I've taught them everything that I could both in technical content and life philosophy.

I must confess to you that I was wavering about if I should continue with my coaching duties, even thinking of abandoning school and my studies because all the shit happened with both IDVRV school and with my own school that I belong to, that all my effort was for nothing and even that I wasn't doing things for you the right way... but in the end thanks to you I realized that I was wrong, I turned you into a (or maybe you already were) nice and kind persons.

It was really moving for my heart that after we came back to the hostel and you instead of going to sleep or going drinking mad to celebrate you started to discuss the problems with me, trying to find a way to do things better even if this was your last time competing together. You even brought a drink for me and paid my return tickets despite I was banbkrupt after paying for all things involved in the competition for you and the other team.

Sometimes I feel envious of people you know -like everyone else, we're humans after all-, being a VJ doesn't make you the protagonist or the heart of the party, you're the one assisting with the ambient and here I'm not the protagonist either. My job with you is a mere supporting role in order to make you reach your goals as a team but you showed that I can be the hero myself even if you are the only ones that think that. To be something that trully matters for a few people, to be something that can really help others is more important than my selfish thoughts.

It took a long time for me to understand it, to realize that even if I'm frustrated most of the time for not being able to do anything that other coachs can offer to their students... I'm just an undergraduate student just like you guys, I don't have money to cover your expenses, nor I have a place to recieve you if you're in need, I do not have the experience as a teacher to explain difficult topics... But you made me realize that I can still do things that no one can, smaller things that can make a day. You have my thanks for teaching me that, it almost feels like I'm your student at those things.

I'll be always endebted to you. To both teams, for everything that you gave me.

If you're reading this now I must tell you that I'm very proud of you boys, you are the best team ever!
