As many of you already know, we, the team "Hot Singles In Your Area" composed by Diego Aldana, Fabián Olivares, Nicolás Pradenas and Erik Regla (coach) from Universidad de Talca have won the first place at our site (Chile) in the past competition ACM ICPC 2016 - South America / South. But we feel that is our duty to raise awareness to the people who were not present during the competition about the true facts ocurred there.
What happened?
A team was disqualified from the contest 15 minutes before starting the competition. The reasons of their disqualification is on the following letter from the contest comitee:

November 12, 2016
To: The ICPC Community
Re: The removal of Problem E from the 2016 Latin American Regional Contest Problem Set

On the eve of November 12, 2016, the date set for the 2016 ICPC Latin American Regional Contests to happen, the contest’s judge and steering committees we were alerted to the following facts, unknown to us:

1- The author of “Problem E – Emission Spectrum” from the 2016 problem set, Nico Lehmann, presented the following paper at the 2016 International Olympiad in Informatics’ Conference. In this paper, the authors discuss the use of a data structure that can be used to efficiently solve Problem E:

“Robinson Castro, Nico Lehmann, Jorge Pérez and Bernardo Subercaseaux, Wavelet Trees for Competitive Programming” (http://ioinformatics.org/conf_content_10.shtml)

They also made some comments on other problems in SPOJ that could be solved using this data structure, including a problem very similar to problem E.

2- One of Nico’s coauthors, Jorge Pérez, is the coach of team “El equipo de Paulsen”, registered to compete in the Regionals. Another coauthor, Robinson Castro, is one of the team’s members.

Upon receiving this news, the Latin-American Contest’s Judge and Steering Committees held joint discussions this morning on the best course of action to take. Without risking any assumptions regarding the integrity of those involved in these issues, it seemed to us that we should act in the interest of preserving the contest’s integrity and reputation, so hardly earned over the last twenty years. The decision was to remove problem E from the problem set and disqualify team “El equipo de Paulsen”.

We do believe the author’s decision to put forward both contributions, paper and problem, was the result of bad judgment. Also, his failure to communicate the Problem Committee of this fact aggravates his fault. It is also a fact that the team would have an unfair advantage over most of the other teams.

We deeply regret all these unfortunate facts that only distract us from the great experience the ICPC has been for many years for our students. We hope to continue deserving the trust of the university community across Latin America.

Prof. Ricardo Dahab/Prof. Ricardo de Oliveira Anido
IC-UNICAMP
Director of Latin American Contests/Director of judging and Problem Committee
Our thoughts
We don't think that this disqualification was fair. For starters, research itself is part of the training process. It's not uncommon for some students to work together with their professors (which usually tend to be their coaches) during their time as undergraduate students.

However, we must remain impartial to both the contestants and the judge desicion.

As from our point of view, if the problem effectively were that the team had an unfair advantage, then the obvious solution is to remove the problem from the problem set. By doing that, thay should not affect their overall performance, there are tons of other problems in the set for them to solve.

But things turned the wrong way, the comitee agreed to both remove the problem and disqualify the team 15 minutes before the competition stated start time. Because of that our team which should be scored 2nd place (because another team was also disqualified but because they didn't met the elegibility requirements they were supposed to be the ones with the 2nd place) ended in first place with only three problems solved.

The paper's author did acted wrong by submitting a problem to the contest? Maybe, we don't know. We don't see anything wrong with submitting a problem which can be solved with that structure, besides it was published and even it's sourcecode was avaliable for all the public.

The comitee did acted wrong by both disqualifying the team and removing the problem? Maybe, but we feel as they did it. If there was effectively a proof of ill intentions behind that act, then they should disqualify the team long before the competition starts and not 15 minutes before. And if the issue were effectively the problem submited by Nicolás Lehman then the logical desicion is to remove it, and both options are sufficient to solve the problem, but not both at the same time. We as a team see this as a message from the comitee that you cannot innovate in teaching, that you cannot share knowledge and even worse, that here in our region Chile have ill intentions with the rest of the contestants. They even dare to write that they hope that we still trust in them, and what for us now? Can we really trust in them now?


We also appreciate the kind gesture from the contest staff to bring up a separate contest server for the disqualified team to compete, isolated from the rest of the contestants in order to test their skills against the problem set. We know that it's not their fault all the things that transpired here, the resolution was not taken by them. It's a heavy burden for them to be the ones that communicate those news.

You must know too that the disqualified team "El Equipo de Paulsen" managed to solve seven problems from a set of eleven, thus, from our point of view making them the true winners of this competition.
Our reaction
We openly said at the award ceremony that we did not deserve such acknowledgement. Nor the prize (that is given to us with a great effort from the contest staff) or the place. We didn't really mean for us to things to end like that, our only reason to participate in this contests is to compete against the best and have fun. The first place is for the winner, to acknowledge their passion and competivity not only against other contestant teams, against themselves too.

The first place is for the best team that pushed the limits further during those hardest 5 hours in the entire year and then proving not only to our country, to our region too that they are the best and they have the right to compete in the world finals against the best teams across the regions.

Those unfair rules made us the first place and we as a team always wanted to win a place... but not in this way. We want to compete, to push our limits further the same way as the other teams, to try harder and then laugh together with other teams while commenting the problem set while enjoying the day.

Maybe other people see that teams treat themselves as rivals between them but it's not true. All teams aspire to be like the best, their competitive spirit motivates them but this competition instance was tainted with this "accident" killing all the competitive spirit that it could had.
Our intent with this letter is to make you, the reader, aware of what happened during the competition, what are our thoughts and what we are really feeling at this very moment.



Diego Aldana
Fabián Olivares
